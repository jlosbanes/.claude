# Example replies

Three real exchanges about a client portal's service request queues, in the current greeting and sign-off.

## 1. Explaining why something happens

Client's message:

> Hi, I just wanted to understand why some new requests submitted by clients through the portal appear in both the "New" and "Assigned" queues. Could you please explain the reason for this and clarify how these requests are being allocated? Thank you for your assistance.

Reply:

```text
Hello, Brightwater Customer Service

Why it's in Assigned:
- The request form's optional "Location Type" field (Building, Level, Tenancy or Common Area) was filled in.
- That makes the portal's automatic routing run when the request is submitted. It confirms who the request is allocated to, records a "Date Assigned" and sets the status to Assigned.

Why it's in New:
- The "Location Type" field was left blank, so the automatic routing step doesn't run.
- The status stays at the default, New. The request is still allocated to the same CSM, and they are still emailed.

Your screenshots show exactly this. The Parks Vic requests in Assigned have Location Type "common_area", and the Trinity College requests in New have it blank. Both sets have a CSM allocated (Sebastian Santana and Kylie Hodge).

Warm Regards,
John Christian L.
```

Why it works:
- The labels answer the two-part question directly, with no lead-in.
- It answers the allocation question too ("still allocated to the same CSM"), so nothing is left hanging.
- It closes with the client's own screenshots, which proves the explanation against what they saw.

## 2. Agreeing to a change and asking for a decision

Client's message:

> Hi John, Thank you for the explanation. Is there anyway they can just be populated into "New" to streamline? Regards,

Reply:

```text
Hello, Maria

Yes, we can do that.

Once the change is made, the portal will work as follows:
- All new requests will start in New and will still be allocated and emailed as they are now.
- They'll move to In Progress automatically the first time they're updated.
- A request will only go to Assigned if someone selects that status manually (clients can do this too).
- Requests currently in Assigned will be moved to New, or to In Progress if they've already been updated.

I'm also thinking of removing Assigned altogether. Let me know if you still have a use for it before I proceed.

Warm Regards,
John Christian L.
```

Why it works:
- The answer comes first in four words.
- The bullets describe what the client will see, not how it's built.
- It deals with existing data ("Requests currently in Assigned…"), which a careful client would otherwise ask about.
- The closing question needs only a short answer and tells her what happens if she says nothing.

## 3. Reporting a finished change

Client's message:

> Hi John, Yes I think removing "Assigned" all together is the best option. Not sure why this was set up initially. Also, moving to "In progress" when a comment is added is something I have been wanting as it seems logical. Thank you,

Reply:

```text
Hello, Maria

Here is now how the application behaves:

- Assigned is removed. Every new request starts in New and is still allocated to its CSM and emailed as before.
- A request moves to In Progress as soon as anyone at Brightwater adds a comment, uploads a photo or updates the request.
- Comments or changes from the client don't move it, so it stays in New until Brightwater picks it up.
- Requests that were in Assigned have been moved to New, or to In Progress if they'd already been worked on.

Warm Regards,
John Christian L.
```

Why it works:
- It describes the finished change as it works now, with no go-live date for her to chase.
- It covers her request (a comment moves a request to In Progress) and the one limit she'd otherwise discover herself (client comments don't).
- It leaves her aside about why Assigned was set up unanswered. It wasn't a question, and the backstory changes nothing for her.
- It accounts for the requests already in Assigned.
